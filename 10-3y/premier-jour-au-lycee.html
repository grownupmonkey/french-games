Pour le prof : how this scene works
  • Students walk a side-view hallway with the arrow keys or the on-screen buttons, read the room number above each door, and press Entrer at the door.
  • Each game builds a new timetable with maths, PE and two other subjects, and new room numbers. The bell gives the time in words (Il est neuf heures vingt-cinq) while the timetable uses digits, so students convert between the two to find the class.
  • Les maths is a number dictation by ear, always including the 70s, 80s and 90s (soixante-quatorze, quatre-vingt-trois, quatre-vingt-dix-sept). Without a French voice, the numbers are shown in words instead. L’EPS is a coach giving vous commands (Sautez ! Courez ! Touchez vos pieds !) against a timer: ten seconds per command in the first round. After each round the coach asks Vous voulez recommencer plus vite ? Students can go faster (6.5, 4.5, then 3 seconds, with more commands each time), retry at the same speed, or stop. Only the first round counts toward the stars; finishing a faster round with every command on time earns the Éclair badge. Other classes use desk commands (Sortez votre cahier, Ouvrez votre livre, Rangez vos ciseaux, Levez la main): students pick the object, then the matching verb in the je form.
  • At recess, students write about their favourite subject with parce que. The checker looks for a subject and a reason, and nudges agreement: ma matière préférée, la classe est intéressante.
  • A first wrong room gets “Regarde ton emploi du temps”; a second gets the answer and the door glows. Mistakes and hints are counted per period.
  • Outcomes practised: 10 C 2.1 (classroom supplies), 10 C 2.2 (course schedules), 10 C 2.3 (routine classroom interactions), 10 C 2.4 (areas of the school), 10 R 2.1e (votre, vos), 10 R 2.8a (classroom commands), 10 R 2.12 (agreement), 10 R 2.17 (parce que).
