    Pour le prof : how this scene works
    • Accents never block progress. Students can use the accent bar or the typing shortcuts on any keyboard, with no settings to change. Missed accents are pointed out after the reply is accepted.
    • Answers are matched loosely: capitals, punctuation, extra words and missing accents are all fine. Meaning-changing accents (a / à, ou / où) get a specific note, and so do missed elisions (je ai → j’ai, je habite → j’habite).
    • Common 10-3Y errors get targeted feedback instead of a generic “wrong”: je suis seize ans, je appelle, je m’appelle est, et tu ?, et vous ? with a classmate (register), je viens de Canada (du), a question without a question mark, and replies typed in English.
    • The first time a student types ? or ! without a space before it, a tip explains the French spacing rule. It is never counted as a mistake.
    • After two missed tries, the hint appears on its own. Hints and retries are counted for the end-of-scene summary.
    • Outcomes practised: 10 C 1.1 and 10 C 1.2 (describing yourself, preferences), 10 A 1–2 (personal and interpersonal functions), 10 R 2.2c (subject pronouns), 10 R 2.4b (asking questions), 10 R 2.10c (avoir + ans), 10 R 3.2 (tu or vous), 10 R 3.4b (French punctuation).
    • Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. Everything runs in the student’s browser.
    • Audio uses one French voice from this computer: checking…. Microsoft Edge usually offers natural-sounding Microsoft voices. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only.
