    Pour le prof : how this scene works
    • Each trip picks a month (janvier or juillet) and three of nine French-speaking cities, with at least one hot stop and one cold or cool one. The weather follows each city’s real climate, so students meet the southern hemisphere: July is cool in Nouméa.
    • The forecast uses il fait chaud / froid / frais / beau, il fait du soleil, il pleut, il neige, il y a du vent, il va pleuvoir and temperatures in words (moins dix degrés). Camille’s postcard adds an activity (beach, fancy dinner, hike, museum) that may need one more item.
    • The suitcase holds six items. A missing item gets a weather-based hint first (Il neige. Et tes pieds ?) and a specific one on the second try. Packing a coat for 30 degrees is accepted but flagged.
    • Every item has a colour that changes each city. Students’ Je porte… sentences are checked for items that are really in the suitcase, the article (une manteau → un manteau), the right colour, and agreement (une écharpe rouge, une veste vert → verte, des bottes noir → noires). Adding parce qu’il fait… earns a bonus.
    • Le vestiaire (after the three cities): four rounds where Camille or Hugo describes an outfit. Each requested piece also appears in a second colour as a decoy, and feedback names the mismatch (La veste est bleue. Camille veut une veste jaune.).
    • Outcomes practised: 10 C 3.2 (clothing), 10 C 3.3 (weather), 10 R 2.1 (gender, number, determiners), 10 R 2.12 (adjective agreement and placement), 10 R 2.6b (il va pleuvoir), 10 R 4.1 (la francophonie: Montréal, Dakar, Genève, Bruxelles, Marrakech, Fort-de-France, Nouméa).
    • Audio uses one French voice from this computer: checking…. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
