    Pour le prof : how this scene works
    • This scene is mostly interpretation: students read or listen to a message, then act on it. Production is choosing the right sentence in a shop and the right question for a stranger.
    • Directions are relative to the way the student is facing, so tourne à droite has to be understood, not matched to a compass. The dotted trail shows the route they actually took.
    • Every game is different: Mamie’s errand and its location clue (à côté de, entre … et …, près de), Camille’s turn-by-turn route, the place the student asks about, the bus time, and Camille’s outfit and spot in the park.
    • Each building has one door. Standing beside the right building on the wrong street gets a pointer to the door’s street, not a penalty. A wrong building gets a reading response (C’est la banque. Tu cherches la poste.). After two wrong buildings the destination glows.
    • In the shop, students choose Je voudrais …, s’il vous plaît over Je veux …, Tu as … ? and a gender error. With M. Bernard, a stranger, they need Excusez-moi, monsieur and vous, not Salut.
    • The bus time comes in words (cinq heures moins le quart) while the timetable uses digits, with et quart and moins le quart decoys. Hints, mistakes and text reveals are counted, and each mission earns up to three stars.
    • Outcomes practised: 10 C 3.1 (shopping venues), 10 C 3.2 (routine purchases), 10 C 3.4 (community services), 10 A 3–4 (referential and directive functions), 10 R 2.1h (au, du), 10 R 2.9b (je voudrais), 10 R 2.12 (colour agreement), 10 R 2.13a (prepositions of location), 10 R 3.2 (tu or vous). Getting around by bus is a 20-3Y context (20 C 3.3), used here as a stretch. The Parc des Buttes-Chaumont is a real Paris park.
    • Audio uses one French voice from this computer: checking…. If it says “none found”, the audio buttons are hidden and the scene works with text only. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
